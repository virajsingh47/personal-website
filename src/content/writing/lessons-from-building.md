I shipped my first real side project last year. Here's what I'd tell myself before I started.

## 1. Talk to users before you write a single line of code

I spent three weeks building a feature nobody asked for. The fix was five conversations with potential users. I should have had those conversations first.

## 2. Launch before you're ready

The version I launched was embarrassing. It was also the version that got me my first ten users and the feedback that made the product worth using.

"Done" is a better teacher than "perfect."

## 3. Distribution is harder than building

I thought the hard part was the product. The hard part is getting people to care. Building is a solved problem — there are tutorials, libraries, frameworks. Distribution is still mostly unsolved and personal.
